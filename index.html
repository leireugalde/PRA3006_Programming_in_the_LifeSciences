Caffeine as a Medicine: Can caffeine support or oppose the effects of specific medical treatments?

Which diseases are treated with medicine that act on the same receptors as coffee? Do these medicines work in the same (agonist) or opposite way (antagonist)?

1. Introduction

Caffeine is the most widely consumed, not only present in coffee but also in tea, energy drinks or even as a supplement in exercise. Consumed by xx% of the population daily (source), it is important to remember that caffeine acts like a drug, targetting specific receptors. It is possible that there is an overlap between the caffeine-targetted receptors, and those targetted by treatment (medicine) of different diseases. This leads us to assume that caffeine could potentially have an impact on the effectivity of the medicine, either acting as a an enhancer (agonist) or opposer (antagonist).
